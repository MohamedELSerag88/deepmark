Here are the brand Archetypes to consider:

The most widely used framework is the 12 Brand Archetypes (Jungian-based model popularized in branding by Pearson & Mark). Some modern strategists expand it to 16–20 variants, but the 12-core system is the global standard.
Below is a clean, MECE breakdown with what you asked for: name, meaning, when to use, customer expectation gap, and examples.

───

1. The Innocent
Description: Pure, optimistic, simple, safe, positive
Best use: When brand promises safety, simplicity, honesty
Customers need: reassurance, clarity, low-risk experience
Expectation gap: “I want things to be simple and safe”
Examples: Dove, Coca-Cola (classic messaging), Disney

───

2. The Sage
Description: Knowledge, wisdom, insight, truth
Best use: Education, AI, research, advisory products
Customers need: understanding, clarity, guidance
Expectation gap: “I want to understand this properly”
Examples: Google, BBC, Harvard, Coursera

───

3. The Explorer
Description: Freedom, discovery, independence, adventure
Best use: Travel, lifestyle, learning, tech exploration
Customers need: growth, self-discovery, new experiences
Expectation gap: “I want to explore beyond limits”
Examples: Jeep, The North Face, Patagonia

Description: Disruption, rule-breaking, revolution

Examples: Tesla (early positioning), Harley-Davidson, Virgin
